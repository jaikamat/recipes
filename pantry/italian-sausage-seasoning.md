### Italian Sausage Seasoning

**Yield:** ~35.5g mild (~44g hot), enough for 680g (1.5 lb) ground pork

A fennel-forward Italian sausage cure scaled by weight rather than spoons, so the salt and sugar land the same every batch. Toasting the fennel is what separates this from a jarred blend.

**Ingredients:**

- 13g sea salt
- 9g granulated sugar
- 9g fennel seeds, toasted
- 2g (3/4 tsp) garlic powder
- 1.5g black pepper, freshly ground
- 1g dried marjoram

_For hot sausage, add:_

- 8g red pepper flakes
- 0.5g cayenne pepper

**Instructions:**

1. Toast the fennel seeds in a dry skillet over medium heat for 2-3 minutes, shaking the pan until they smell sweet and turn a shade darker, then tip them onto a plate to cool completely
2. Crush the cooled fennel lightly in a mortar or under the flat of a knife, leaving some seeds whole so they still pop against the meat
3. Combine the fennel with the remaining ingredients in a small bowl and whisk until evenly distributed
4. Mix the full batch into 680g (1.5 lb) ground pork, working it until the meat turns tacky, then cover and rest in the fridge for 8 hours before forming and cooking

**Storage:** Store in an airtight jar in a cool, dark place. The salt and sugar keep indefinitely, but toasted fennel fades — mix within 3 months for the best aroma.

**Notes:** Salt is measured by weight, so kosher, sea, or table salt all work at 13g. The 2g of garlic powder comes from dehydration math rather than the usual "1/8 tsp per clove" swap — 7g of fresh garlic is about 2.5g of solids once the water is gone, and the spoon conversion lands under 1g, which reads noticeably underseasoned in sausage. Drop to 1.5g for a milder garlic note. Garlic powder rehydrates in the mix and picks up a slightly sharper, more cured edge than fresh; the 8-hour rest blooms it fully, so there is no need to add extra to compensate.
