### Dried Herb Chimichurri

**Yield:** ~215g (just under 1 cup; 15 servings, 1 TBSP each), enough to marinate 900g (2 lb) of chicken with 30g (2 TBSP) reserved for serving
**Per serving:** 66 cal | 0.2g protein | 7g fat | 0.8g carbs
**Per 100g:** 472 cal | 1.4g protein | 50g fat | 6g carbs (~14g per serving)

A chimichurri built entirely from the pantry — dried herbs and jarred garlic, no bunch of parsley required. The dried herbs get bloomed in warm salted water before any oil goes near them, which is the whole trick: dropped straight into oil they stay papery and never give anything up.

**Ingredients:**

- 4g (3 TBSP) dried parsley
- 2g (1 TBSP) dried cilantro
- 2g (2 tsp) dried oregano
- 4g (1 tsp) sea salt
- 1g (1/4 tsp) MSG
- 30g (2 TBSP) warm water
- 16g (1 TBSP) garlic paste
- 2g (1 tsp) red pepper flakes
- 45g (3 TBSP) white vinegar
- 1g (1/4 tsp) granulated sugar
- 0.6g (1/4 tsp) black pepper, coarsely ground
- 107g (1/2 cup) olive oil

**Instructions:**

1. Combine the parsley, cilantro, oregano, sea salt, and MSG in a medium bowl. Stir in the warm water until the salt and MSG dissolve, then leave it 5 minutes to rehydrate the herbs — they should go from papery to soft and dark green
2. Stir in the garlic paste, red pepper flakes, vinegar, sugar, and black pepper, mixing until the sugar has dissolved
3. Pour in the olive oil and whisk hard until it comes together into a loose, broken sauce. It will not emulsify and is not supposed to
4. Cover and let it sit at room temperature for 30 minutes so the flavors meld, then taste and adjust the salt
5. Before it touches any raw chicken, spoon 30g (2 TBSP) into a separate small container and refrigerate. This is the serving sauce, and once the rest has been in a bag with raw chicken there is no getting it back
6. Put 900g (2 lb) chicken in a gallon zip-top bag with the remaining sauce, press out the air, massage to coat, and refrigerate 2-4 hours
7. Take the reserved sauce out 20-30 minutes before serving so the oil loosens, stir it, and drizzle it over the cooked chicken

**Storage:** Sauce that has never touched raw chicken keeps about a week in a sealed jar in the fridge. The olive oil goes solid and cloudy when cold — let the jar sit out 20-30 minutes and stir before using.

**Notes:**

- Counting this honestly: the per-serving line is the sauce itself, which is the right number for the reserved drizzle because you eat all of it. It is the wrong number for the marinade, because most of that stays in the bag. Of the ~185g that goes in with the chicken, meat typically holds onto 10-20% by weight, so a 225g portion of the finished chicken carries roughly 60-80 cal and 6-8g fat from the sauce — the 2 TBSP drizzle split four ways plus whatever clung on. Dividing the whole batch by four portions would put it near 250 cal, which overcounts by about 3x
- Nearly all of it is the olive oil: 107g of the 215g batch, and 946 of the 1,012 calories. The herbs, garlic, and vinegar together come to about 65 cal for the entire batch. If you want this lighter, oil is the only lever that matters — dropping to 80g (1/3 cup) takes the batch to ~770 cal and still coats 2 lb of chicken, though the sauce gets sharper and needs a pinch more sugar
- Marinating time: 2-4 hours is the sweet spot. Past about 6 hours the vinegar works too far into the surface of the meat and the texture turns chalky rather than tender
- Salt is measured by weight, so kosher, sea, or table all work at 4g — worth knowing because a teaspoon of Diamond Crystal and a teaspoon of Morton differ by nearly double. Push to 6g for a more deeply seasoned marinade; most of the salt stays behind in the bag rather than on the chicken
- MSG is doing real work here. Dried herbs read flatter than fresh, and 1g is what keeps this from tasting like seasoned vinegar and oil. It still works without it, just thinner
- This does not pretend to be fresh chimichurri. What it gives up is the grassy top note of a fresh parsley bunch; what it buys is a sauce that is always available and keeps a week instead of two days
- Doubling works cleanly and the sauce keeps, but only double the batch if you will actually use it — the bloomed herbs are best in the first few days
