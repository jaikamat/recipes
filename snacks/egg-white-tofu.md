### Egg White Tofu

**Servings:** 4 (~150g each)
**Per serving:** 66 cal | 14g protein | 0g fat | 1g carbs
**Per 100g:** 43 cal | 9g protein | 0g fat | 1g carbs (~150g per serving)

A soy-free stand-in for medium-firm tofu, in the tradition of Japanese tamago dofu (egg tofu). A carton of egg whites gently set in a water bath into a sliceable, jelly-like block — cube it and braise it anywhere tofu goes. One batch makes ~600g, enough to replace the 400g of soft tofu in Mapo Tofu with some left over.

**Ingredients:**

- 454g egg whites (one 1 lb carton)
- 150g Kirkland chicken bone broth (or water for a neutral block)
- 3g sea salt
- 1g MSG

**Instructions:**

1. Heat the oven to 300°F and put a kettle of water on to boil. Lightly oil a loaf pan (~8.5x4.5 inch) or line it with parchment — the loaf shape gives proper tofu-cube depth; an 8x8 works but yields thinner cubes
2. Whisk the egg whites, broth, salt, and MSG together gently, stirring rather than beating — you want them combined, not foamy
3. Strain through a fine-mesh sieve into the pan and skim or drag off any surface bubbles with a paper towel corner. Cover the pan tightly with foil — the cover is what prevents the skin
4. Set the loaf pan in a roasting pan, pour boiling water halfway up its sides, and bake 45-60 minutes until just set with a uniform jiggle, 165°F internal. A stovetop steamer over the barest simmer works equally well; hard heat causes honeycomb bubbles, so keep it gentle either way
5. Cool to room temperature, then refrigerate at least 2 hours — the gel firms as it chills and slices much cleaner cold
6. Unmold, slice into 1-inch cubes, and use like medium-firm tofu: add to braises and sauces in the last few minutes of cooking and stir gently

**Freeze:** Not recommended — egg gels weep and turn spongy when frozen.

**Storage:** Fridge up to 5 days in an airtight container, covered or submerged in water like tofu.

**Notes:**

- Why it works: egg white protein is itself a thermal gel — no gelatin, xanthan, or agar needed. Xanthan only thickens (it never sets), and gelatin melts around 95°F, so gelatin cubes would dissolve in a hot braise. Gentle, covered, moist heat is the whole trick: an uncovered bake dries the surface into a skin
- Firmness dial: the broth is the tenderizer. Omit it entirely for the firmest, bounciest block; 150g gives a braisable medium-firm; up to 225g approaches soft/silken and is best spooned rather than cubed
- Culinary precedent: Japanese tamago dofu and the commercial "egg tofu" tubes in Asian markets are the same idea with whole eggs and dashi — they're routinely cubed, fried, and braised in Chinese cooking. Egg whites alone set firmer and leaner
- Broth adds a light savory backbone; use water plus a pinch more salt and MSG if you want it flavor-neutral
- A silicone mold (e.g. a 4-cup Souper Cubes rectangle) works as well as metal and releases cleaner — no oil or parchment needed. Set the mold in the roasting pan before filling (full of liquid it's floppy), cover with foil rather than the plastic lid (not oven-safe), and expect the set to take 10-15 minutes longer since silicone insulates. The jiggle/165°F doneness test doesn't change
- Made for the Mapo Tofu in dinner/ — swap 400g of cubes in for the soft tofu, added off a hard boil in the final 2-3 minutes
