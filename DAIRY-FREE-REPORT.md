# Dairy-Free & Soy-Free Recipe Report

A review of all 45 recipes in the collection for **dairy and soy** content, prepared to support an elimination while breastfeeding an infant with a suspected cow's milk protein allergy (CMPA). Because soy is a common co-allergen with dairy in infants, the report covers both — see the **combined dairy-free + soy-free** section at the end, which is the actionable list for your goal of cooking without either.

> **A note on strictness for infant CMPA**
> For a strict elimination, three "hidden" or borderline dairy sources matter and are flagged throughout this report:
> - **Ghee** — clarified butter. Most milk protein is removed, but trace casein/whey can remain. Pediatric allergists commonly recommend avoiding it during a strict elimination. It's a trivial swap (olive or neutral oil), so ghee-only recipes are listed as *easily modifiable* rather than dairy-free.
> - **Whey & casein protein powders** — whey is milk-derived and is dairy. Swap for a plant protein (pea, soy, rice). Unflavored/vanilla pea protein is the closest 1:1.
> - **Chocolate chips** — standard chips (e.g., Toll House) contain milk fat/milk solids and often carry "may contain milk." Use certified dairy-free dark chocolate (look for ≥70% with no milk in the ingredients, or an explicit dairy-free label).
> - **Always check labels** on prepared items: marinara, pesto, boxed stuffing, cake/pudding mixes, and "buttery" products vary by brand.

> **A note on soy sources**
> Soy hides in more places than dairy. The ones that matter here:
> - **Soy sauce / dark soy sauce / tamari** — obvious, but central to the Chinese recipes. Swap with **coconut aminos** (less salty and a touch sweeter — add a pinch of salt to compensate). Dark soy is mostly for color; mimic with a few drops of molasses.
> - **Tofu** — in two recipes it *is* the dish (mapo tofu, marinara baked tofu); not substitutable without changing what you're making.
> - **Oyster sauce** — many brands contain soy (and wheat); soy-free versions exist but are niche.
> - **Soy lecithin in chocolate chips** — present in nearly all standard chips. *Allergy nuance:* highly refined lecithin is tolerated by most soy-allergic people and is often the tiniest trace. For a **strict** elimination, use a soy-lecithin-free brand (Enjoy Life, Pascha, or a plain 100% bar) — these are usually dairy-free too, so one product solves both.
> - **Soybean oil** — the default in generic "vegetable/neutral oil," boxed mixes, and instant roux. Refined soybean oil is also usually tolerated, but to be strict use **olive or avocado oil** (which is what I'd reach for in the ghee→oil swaps anyway).
> - **Boxed mixes** (cake, pudding, stuffing) and **Worcestershire** — common soy carriers; check labels or go scratch.

The four shopping moves that unlock the most recipes:
- **Pea protein** in place of whey (cornbread, protein bars, PB spread) — keeps them dairy- *and* soy-free.
- **Olive/avocado oil** for every ghee and "neutral oil" swap (avoids both dairy and soybean oil).
- **One soy-lecithin-free, dairy-free chocolate** (Enjoy Life / Pascha / 100% bar) for all five chocolate recipes.
- **Coconut aminos** for soy sauce across the Chinese dishes; **coconut yogurt** as the non-soy creamy stand-in for cottage cheese.

The genuinely off-limits dishes are the cheese-defined ones (ricotta pancakes, the cheese soups/pasta/pizza, the cream-cheese bakes) and the two tofu dishes — there's no clean swap that keeps them recognizable.
