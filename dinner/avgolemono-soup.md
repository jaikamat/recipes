### Avgolemono Soup

**Servings:** 8 (about 380g each)
**Per serving:** 203 cal | 29g protein | 3g fat | 27g carbs
**Per 100g:** 53 cal | 8g protein | 1g fat | 7g carbs (~380g per serving)

A lean, lemony Greek-style chicken and pasta soup built on unsalted bone broth. High-protein pasta and 98% fat free ground chicken breast hold a bowl right around 200 calories while still landing 29g of protein.

**Ingredients:**

- 14g ghee
- 110g onion, diced (about 1 medium)
- 102g celery, diced (about 1 cup)
- 75g carrots, diced
- 15g (1 TBSP) garlic paste
- 454g (1 lb) ground chicken breast (98% fat free, Perdue)
- 1920g (8 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 2 bay leaves
- 2 1/2 tsp dried dill, divided
- 1/2 tsp dried oregano
- 1/2 tsp dried thyme
- 2g (1 tsp) ground cumin
- 23g (about 4 tsp) chicken bouillon paste (Better Than Bouillon)
- 7g sea salt
- 250g Carbe Diem elbow pasta
- 2g (1 tsp) lemon zest
- 48g lemon juice, from about 1 lemon
- Black pepper to taste

**Instructions:**

1. Brown the ground chicken in a dry frypan over medium-high heat, breaking it into small crumbles as it cooks, until it is cooked through and starting to catch on the pan. Season lightly and set aside — 98% fat free breast renders almost nothing, so let it sit undisturbed to build color before you stir
2. Melt the ghee in a dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add the carrots, celery, and onion
3. Sauté the vegetables until they begin to soften, about 5 minutes. Add the garlic paste and stir continuously just until it becomes fragrant, about 1 minute. Add 2 tsp of the dill along with the oregano, thyme, and cumin and stir another 30 seconds, until they smell toasted — blooming dried herbs in the hot fat pulls far more out of them than dropping them into broth does, and the cumin in particular needs the fat to open up
4. Add the broth and the bay leaves. Simmer about 5 minutes, or until the vegetables are tender
5. Stir the bouillon paste and the salt straight into the simmering pot and taste. Kirkland bone broth is nearly unsalted and mild, so the bouillon is doing two jobs here — about half the seasoning and all of the roasted-chicken depth. These amounts are the tested sweet spot; adjust from here rather than starting lower
6. Add the browned chicken and any juices it gave up
7. Bring the soup back up to a simmer and add the pasta. Cook until al dente, about 7 minutes, stirring so it doesn't sink and stick
8. Kill the heat and fish out the bay leaves. Stir in the lemon zest, the lemon juice, and the last 1/2 tsp of dill. Taste and adjust the lemon — one lemon is the starting point, but they vary enough in acidity that the only real measure is whether the soup tastes bright rather than flat. Finish with black pepper

**Freeze:** Cool completely and portion into containers for up to 2 months. Better yet, freeze the soup before the pasta goes in and cook fresh pasta into the thawed base.

**Thaw:** Overnight in the fridge, or straight from frozen on the stove over low heat with the lid on.

**Reheat:** Gently on the stove or microwave covered, stirring halfway. Add broth or water to loosen — the pasta keeps drinking liquid. Finish with a fresh squeeze of lemon, which fades in storage.

**Serve:** With black pepper and a little more lemon. Good with crusty bread or a spoonful of Greek yogurt stirred in for body.

**Storage:** Fridge, 4 days. The pasta softens and the soup tightens noticeably by day 2.

**Notes:**

- No egg, despite the name — traditional avgolemono is thickened by tempering 2-3 eggs beaten with the lemon juice into a ladleful of hot broth, then stirring that back in off the heat. That version is the real thing and adds about 9 cal, 0.8g protein, and 0.6g fat per serving per egg — roughly 27 cal, 2.4g protein, and 1.8g fat for three. This one skips it and stays a clear, brothy soup
- The pasta is the whole macro story. Carbe Diem elbows run 14g protein and 24g fiber per 56g dry, so 250g contributes 36g protein and most of the carb count is fiber — regular semolina elbows would drop protein by about 3g per serving and add roughly 20 cal. Orzo is the classic shape here if you want tradition over macros
- Farro instead of pasta: use 200g (about 1 cup) semi-pearled farro in place of the 250g elbows, and add another 240g (1 cup) broth to cover what it drinks. It goes in at step 4 with the broth and herbs, not at step 7 — semi-pearled farro wants 25-30 minutes to come tender with a chew, so start it early and add the chicken at step 6 as written. Lands around 237 cal | 29g protein | 3g fat | 23g carbs per serving. Protein holds because the extra cup of broth backfills what the lower-protein grain gives up
- The farro version reads lower in total carbs but is the bigger carb load in practice. Carbe Diem elbows are 43% fiber by weight, so 250g is only about 71g of net carbs; 200g of farro is closer to 123g. If you are watching net carbs rather than calories, the pasta is the version to make. What farro buys you is texture — it holds a distinct grain for days where the pasta goes soft by day 2, and the starch it sheds gives the broth some body, which is a fair stand-in for the egg this recipe skips
- About 11g of the 29g protein per serving is collagen from the bone broth, which is incomplete. Against a leucine target the real figure is closer to 17g — the chicken and pasta are doing the useful work
- Ghee is the only added fat and there is only 14g of it. Olive oil works identically if that is what is open; the dish is lean enough that neither choice moves the macro line much
- 23g bouillon and 7g salt is the tested spot. That is roughly 5,360mg of added sodium against 5,510mg for the 14g of straight salt this started as, so it lands within about 3% — call it sodium-neutral, near 800mg per serving all in. 6g salt also tastes seasoned and saves about 400mg across the pot, which is the version to make if the sodium number matters more than the last bit of pop
- The split is deliberate — bouillon runs 680mg sodium per tsp against 393mg per gram of salt, so the paste and the salt land within a couple hundred milligrams of each other, about half the seasoning apiece. Pushing the bouillon further and dropping the salt entirely gets you the same sodium, but Better Than Bouillon is roast-forward enough that past about 5 tsp the soup stops tasting of lemon and dill and starts tasting of bouillon
- No bouillon in the house? Go back to 14g sea salt and nothing else — the full 14g, not 7g, since without the glutamates propping it up the soup needs the salt back. You lose the savory backbone but not the dish, and it drops about 7 cal per serving
- Bouillon adds savor, not body. 23g of it puts under 2g of fat into the whole pot, and body comes from fat, starch, or egg — so if this reads thin to you, the tempered-egg version above is the actual fix, not more paste. For pure umami without the roast note, 2g MSG against 12g salt is the cleaner lever
- Pasta in the pot will keep swelling and drinking broth in the fridge. For meal prep, cook the pasta separately and add it per bowl, or accept that day 3 is closer to a stew
- Cumin is the piece this soup was missing. It is borrowed from the Greek lemon rice, where 1g against 300g of dry rice is what keeps the lemon and dill from reading thin — cumin is earthy and slightly sweet, and it sits underneath acid the way oregano and thyme do not. 2g across roughly 3kg of finished soup is about half the dose per gram that the rice carries, so this is firmly a background note — it reads as depth rather than as cumin. Matching the rice outright would be closer to 4g, and that is the direction to go if it does not register at all; past about 5g the soup starts drifting toward Middle Eastern lemon-chicken territory rather than Greek
- Cumin has to go in at step 3 with the other dried herbs, not into the broth. Its aroma compounds are fat-soluble, so a dose that only ever simmers in liquid tastes dusty and raw — the same reason the dried dill gets bloomed
- Fresh thyme instead of dried: use 4-5 whole sprigs, added at step 4 with the bay leaves and fished out with them at step 8, and skip the 1/2 tsp dried. Thyme is the one herb here that improves in a long simmer rather than fading, so the sprigs go in early — the opposite of the dill. Strip and chop the leaves off one sprig and stir them in at the end if you want the aroma on top too. Nothing else changes, and it does not move the macro line
- The dill is split on purpose: 2 tsp bloomed in the fat at step 3 for the base note, 1/2 tsp raw at the end for the top note. Dried dill loses its volatile side to heat, so a dose that only ever simmers reads flat no matter how much of it goes in — the finishing half teaspoon is what makes the soup smell like dill when the bowl hits the table
- Fresh dill is still the better version if you have it. Use about 5g chopped, all of it stirred in at step 8, and skip both doses of dried — fresh is grassy and bright where dried is hay-like. Dried holds up better through freezing though, so this version reheats closer to how it tasted on day one
- Dried dill loses potency fast on the shelf. If yours has been open a year, smell it before committing — if it reads like dust rather than dill, take the bloom up to 1 TBSP and keep the finishing 1/2 tsp as is
- Lemon goes in off the heat on purpose. Simmering fresh lemon juice dulls it into something flat and slightly bitter, and the zest carries the aroma that the juice alone does not
- A whole lemon (about 48g of juice) is the working amount, but treat it as a range rather than a measurement. Supermarket lemons swing widely in acidity depending on variety and how long they have sat, and 8 servings of broth is a lot of volume to move — start with one lemon, taste, and keep going if it reads flat. Doubling it costs 5 cal and 2g carbs across the whole pot, so there is no macro reason to hold back
