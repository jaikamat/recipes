### Greek Lemon Rice

**Servings:** 4 (205g each)
**Per serving:** 340 cal | 11g protein | 4g fat | 63g carbs
**Per 100g:** 166 cal | 6g protein | 2g fat | 31g carbs (~205g per serving)

Rice cooker lemon rice built entirely on dried herbs. The dill and oregano get
bloomed in hot oil in the microwave — no pan, one glass measuring cup — so they
taste like far more than a shake from the jar.

**Ingredients:**

- 300g basmati rice, uncooked
- 465g Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 45g lemon juice (about 1 large lemon)
- 14g olive oil
- 12g garlic paste
- 6g lemon zest (about 1 large lemon)
- 5g sea salt
- 3g onion powder
- 2g MSG
- 2g dried dill
- 1.5g dried oregano
- 1g ground cumin

**Instructions:**

1. Rinse basmati rice until the water runs clear, then leave it to soak in cool water while you build the base
2. Combine olive oil and garlic paste in a 2-cup glass measuring cup and microwave uncovered for 60 seconds, until the oil is visibly shimmering and the garlic smells cooked rather than raw
3. Stir in sea salt, MSG, onion powder, dried dill, dried oregano, and cumin and microwave 20 seconds more, until the oil smells strongly of herbs. This is the whole trick — the oil is well past 250°F, which is what pulls the flavor out of dried herbs the way a pan would. Don't push past 30 seconds here, since onion powder is fine enough to scorch
4. Stir the lemon zest and lemon juice into the hot oil and let it sit for a minute
5. Drain the rice thoroughly and add it to the rice cooker, then scrape in every bit of the seasoned oil and add the chicken bone broth
6. Stir once to distribute and cook on the regular white rice setting
7. Fluff with a fork and let rest 5 minutes, then taste and add another squeeze of lemon if it needs brightening

**Freeze:** In four 1-cup molds overnight, then transfer to bags.

**Reheat:** Microwave 2-3 minutes until heated through, with a splash of water if it looks dry.

**Serve:** Alongside grilled chicken, meatballs, or anything with yogurt sauce.

**Storage:** Airtight in the fridge up to 5 days.

**Notes:** Seasoning lands at 5g salt and 2g MSG for roughly 820g of finished
rice — about 0.6% salt, which reads well-seasoned rather than salty. The MSG does
double duty here: it adds savory depth, but it also makes the salt already
present taste stronger, so the two together go further than 5g of salt alone
would. If it still comes up flat, take the salt to 6g before touching the MSG
again — past about 3g the MSG starts tasting like itself instead of amplifying
the dish.

Dried dill is the workhorse — 2g is roughly 2 tsp, about triple what a
1:3 dried-to-fresh swap would suggest, because dried dill fades in a long cook.
Bottled lemon juice works if you skip the zest, but drop the juice to 40g and add
1g extra dried dill to compensate for the missing aromatic oils. A pinch of dried
mint (about 0.5g) rounds it out if you have it.

Full dump-and-go version: skip the microwave entirely and put everything in the
cooker at once. It still works, but the herbs stay flat and a little dusty. The
80 seconds of microwaving is the single highest-return step in the recipe.

Onion powder stands in for 60g of sliced onion here. It brings the savory depth
but not the sweetness real onion develops as it softens, so the dish reads a
little sharper — the extra lemon at the end covers for it. Garlic powder (2g) can
replace the garlic paste on the same logic if you'd rather keep it fully
shelf-stable; cut the first microwave stage to 30 seconds if you do, since
there's no water left to cook off.

Use a tall, wide vessel for the blooming step and leave it uncovered — a narrow
cup can spit hot oil.
