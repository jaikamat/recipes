### Turkey & Dumplings

**Servings:** 4
**Per serving:** 649 cal | 41g protein | 24g fat | 67g carbs
**Per 100g:** 113 cal | 7g protein | 4g fat | 12g carbs (~575g per serving)

Chicken and dumplings built on browned ground turkey and canned biscuit dough. The turkey never simmers — it goes back in at the end — and the bone broth supplies the body that stewed chicken thighs normally would.

**Ingredients:**

_Stew:_

- 385g (0.85 lb, half a 1.7 lb packet) ground turkey (93/7)
- 15ml (1 TBSP) olive oil
- 150g (1 medium) onion, diced
- 130g (2 medium) carrots, sliced into 5mm coins
- 100g (2 ribs) celery, sliced
- 33g (2 TBSP) garlic paste
- 24g (3 TBSP) 00 flour
- 1.2L (5 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 12g (2 tsp) chicken bouillon paste (Better Than Bouillon)
- 1.5 tsp dried thyme
- 1 tsp sage, rubbed (or poultry seasoning)
- 1 small bay leaf
- 1/4 tsp sea salt, for the turkey only — see Notes
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes

_Dumplings:_

- 456g (1 can, 8 biscuits) Annie's Organic Flaky Biscuits, each quartered

**Instructions:**

1. Heat the olive oil in a wide Dutch oven or 5-quart pot over medium-high — hot enough to sizzle, not smoking. Add the turkey in one layer, season with the 1/4 tsp sea salt, and leave it alone for 4-5 minutes before breaking it up. You want real brown color on at least half of it; that browning is most of the flavor a whole chicken would have given you. Break it into coarse crumbles, cook 2 minutes more, and scrape it into a bowl
2. Drop the heat to medium. Add the onion, carrot, and celery and cook 7-8 minutes, until the onion is translucent and the fond on the bottom of the pot has loosened. Stir in the garlic paste, thyme, sage, black pepper, and red pepper flakes; cook 1 minute. Do not salt the vegetables
3. Sprinkle the flour over the vegetables and stir constantly for 90 seconds — it should coat everything and smell toasty, not raw
4. Whisk the bouillon paste into about 240ml of the cold bone broth until dissolved, then pour that plus the remaining broth into the pot in three additions, scraping the bottom and stirring smooth after each. Add the bay leaf, bring to a bare simmer, and cook uncovered 10 minutes, until the carrots are just tender
5. Return the turkey and any accumulated juices to the pot. Taste the broth now. It should be a touch under-seasoned — the biscuits release a lot of salt as they cook. Add more bouillon paste 1/2 tsp at a time if it's flat, but stop before it tastes right on its own
6. Adjust the heat so the surface is at a lazy simmer, not a boil. Drop the biscuit quarters in one at a time, spacing them across the surface. They should sit in the liquid, not submerged
7. Cover and cook 15 minutes without lifting the lid. The tops cook by trapped steam; every peek costs you rise. They're done when a quarter pulled from the center is matte and set through rather than glossy and wet
8. Kill the heat, fish out the bay leaf, and rest 5 minutes uncovered before serving. The broth tightens noticeably as the biscuit starch sets

**Freeze:** Not recommended as assembled — cooked biscuit dumplings go pasty and collapse on thaw. To freeze, stop after step 5 and portion the stew base into Souper Cubes for up to 3 months, then add fresh dumplings when you reheat it. A can of biscuit dough is cheap and the 15-minute steam is the same either way.

**Reheat:** Covered, over low heat, with a splash of broth to loosen. From frozen base, thaw overnight in the fridge, bring back to a lazy simmer, then drop in the quartered biscuits and steam 15 minutes covered.

**Storage:** Fridge, 3 days. The dumplings keep absorbing broth, so it firms up toward a casserole by day 2.

**Notes:**

- Salt strategy: the biscuits carry roughly 570mg sodium each, so the can alone puts ~1,140mg into every serving before anything else. Bone broth is nearly unsalted (95mg/cup), which is why it's the liquid, and the bouillon paste is the only seasoning lever — it brings roasted-chicken depth and glutamates, not just salt. Season at step 5, not before. As written this lands near 1,750mg per serving
- Protein caveat for tracking: 55g of the 164g total is collagen from the bone broth, which is incomplete (no tryptophan, low in several essentials). If you're counting toward a leucine/complete-protein target, the honest number is closer to 27g per serving, not 41g
- Olive oil browns fine here, but keep it at medium-high rather than ripping hot. If it's a peppery finishing EVOO, expect some bitterness at browning temperature — regular or light olive oil is the better pick for step 1
- Quarter the biscuits — whole ones stay gummy in the middle at this depth of liquid
- No flour on hand: skip step 3 and whisk 12g (1.5 TBSP) cornstarch into 60ml cold broth, stirring it in at the end of step 4
- 99/1 turkey will read dry and chalky no matter what you do with it. Use 93/7 or 85/15
- Protein knob: the full 770g packet with broth scaled to 1.6L gives 5 servings at roughly 650 cal | 51g protein | 25g fat | 53g carbs. Same can of biscuits
- Turkey & Slick Dumplings is the leaner rebuild of this — rolled pizza dough instead of canned biscuits, at 523 cal | 55g protein | 16g fat per serving and about a third the sodium
