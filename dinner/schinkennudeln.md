### Schinkennudeln (One-Pot Ham & Cheese Noodle Bake)

**Servings:** 6
**Per serving:** 537 cal | 45g protein | 18g fat | 51g carbs
**Per 100g:** 140 cal | 12g protein | 5g fat | 13g carbs (~385g per serving)

The Austrian baked version of this is _Schinkenfleckerl_ — noodles and ham bound in an egg-and-quark custard, baked until the top sets and browns. Blended cottage cheese stands in for the quark, which is both the authentic move and where most of the protein comes from. Everything happens in one Dutch oven: the pasta cooks in its own broth by absorption, the custard goes in off-heat, and the pot goes straight into the oven. Protein is a third of the calories here, roughly double what a standard Schinkennudeln lands at.

**Ingredients:**

_Pot:_

- 340g (12 oz) protein pasta, short shape — rotini, cavatappi, or broken egg noodles
- 340g (12 oz) ham, cut into 1cm dice (Black Forest deli, lean)
- 15ml (1 TBSP) olive oil
- 150g onion, diced (about 1 medium)
- 130g carrots, grated on the large holes of a box grater (about 2 medium)
- 12g (2 TBSP) garlic paste
- 720ml (3 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 150g (1 cup) frozen peas
- 140g (5 oz) spinach
- 1 tsp dried marjoram
- 1/2 tsp caraway seed, lightly crushed (optional but very German)
- 1 tsp sea salt
- 1 tsp black pepper, freshly ground

_Custard:_

- 340g (1.5 cups) 4% cottage cheese
- 3 eggs
- 10g (2 tsp) Dijon mustard
- 1/4 tsp nutmeg, freshly grated
- 100g (3.5 oz) Gruyère, grated (or aged Emmental)

**Instructions:**

1. Heat the oven to 375°F (190°C) with a rack in the upper third
2. Blend the cottage cheese completely smooth — blender or immersion blender, 30-45 seconds. Do this first and set it aside. Unblended curds will read as lumps in the finished bake no matter how long it cooks
3. Heat the olive oil in a 5-quart Dutch oven over medium-high. Add the ham in one layer and leave it alone for 3 minutes, until the edges brown and curl. Stir once, give it another 2 minutes, then scrape it into a bowl. Deli ham is wet and lean; if you stir it constantly it steams grey and you lose the whole point
4. Drop to medium. Add the onion, carrot, and 1/2 tsp of the salt and cook 6 minutes, until the onion is translucent and the carrot has softened into the fond. Stir in the garlic paste, marjoram, caraway, black pepper, and remaining salt; cook 1 minute
5. Add the dry pasta and stir for 60 seconds to coat it in the fat and toast it slightly
6. Pour in 480ml of the broth, scraping the bottom clean. Bring to a simmer and cook uncovered, stirring every 2 minutes so nothing catches, adding the remaining 240ml in splashes as it is absorbed. Cook to 3 minutes short of al dente — it should still have a firm chalky center, because it keeps cooking in the oven. The pot should be glossy, not soupy: a spoon dragged across the bottom leaves a trail that fills in slowly
7. Stir in the peas and the spinach in two or three handfuls, letting each wilt before adding the next. Return the ham and any juices. Kill the heat
8. Let the pot sit off-heat for 4 minutes. This is the step that decides whether the dish is silky or scrambled. While it rests, whisk the blended cottage cheese, eggs, Dijon, and nutmeg together, then whisk a ladleful of the hot liquid from the pot into that bowl to temper it
9. Fold the custard through the pasta until evenly distributed. Smooth the top and scatter the Gruyère over it
10. Bake uncovered 20-25 minutes, until the custard is set and no longer jiggles when you nudge the pot. Switch to the broiler for 2-3 minutes to blister the cheese, watching it the whole time
11. Rest 10 minutes before serving. It slices rather than slumps if you wait

**Freeze:** Freezes acceptably, which is unusual for an egg-bound bake — the cottage cheese stabilizes it. Freeze in portions, never as one block; the center of a block never reheats evenly. Expect slightly softer pasta.

**Thaw:** Overnight in the fridge.

**Reheat:** Covered at 325°F (165°C) for 15 minutes, or microwave at 70% power with a damp paper towel over the top. Full-power microwaving will weep the custard.

**Storage:** Fridge, 4 days. This is a genuinely good meal-prep dish — the egg custard means it sets firm when cold and reheats without going greasy the way a béchamel-based bake does. Portion into containers while still warm.

**Notes:**

- The custard is the only real failure point. Eggs curdle above ~160°F (72°C), and a Dutch oven holds heat brutally well — the 4-minute rest plus tempering in step 8 is not optional. If you are nervous, take the pot off the burner entirely and set it on a cold trivet
- Grating the carrot rather than dicing it is deliberate: it disappears into the custard and adds sweetness and volume without anyone picking around orange coins
- Peas are worth adding. They are the cheapest protein in the dish (about 8g across the pot) and they hold their shape where the spinach collapses to nothing
- **Volume knob:** another 150g of spinach and 100g of peas adds about 40g per serving for under 20 cal. This bakes fine at that volume in a 5-quart pot
- **Protein knob:** swapping in 200g of shredded rotisserie chicken alongside the ham takes it to about 53g protein per serving. Or use a chickpea/lentil pasta instead of wheat protein pasta for about +1g per serving, though the texture goes softer and you will want to pull it 4 minutes short in step 6 instead of 3
- Regular semolina pasta drops it to about 42g protein per serving. Still respectable — the cottage cheese and ham are doing most of the work
- Gruyère is the one place not to economize. 100g of something sharp and nutty beats 200g of mild cheese at half the fat
- Fleckerl (square flakes) are the traditional shape and nearly impossible to find in the US. Rotini traps the custard better than anything else in a normal grocery store
