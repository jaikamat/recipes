### Turkey & Slick Dumplings

**Servings:** 5
**Per serving:** 523 cal | 55g protein | 16g fat | 39g carbs
**Per 100g:** 97 cal | 10g protein | 3g fat | 7g carbs (~540g per serving)

The leaner rebuild of Turkey & Dumplings. Canned biscuit dough is swapped for the margherita pizza dough rolled thin and cut into strips — Southern-style slick dumplings, chewy rather than fluffy. Dropping the biscuits cuts the fat by two thirds and the sodium by about 60%, and the vital wheat gluten carries the dumplings to 22% protein by dry weight.

**Ingredients:**

_Dumpling dough (one batch of the margherita dough, salt reduced):_

- 180g 00 flour
- 50g vital wheat gluten
- 3g active dry yeast
- 2g sea salt
- 170g water

_Stew:_

- 770g (1.7 lb packet) ground turkey (93/7)
- 15ml (1 TBSP) olive oil
- 150g (1 medium) onion, diced
- 130g (2 medium) carrots, sliced into 5mm coins
- 100g (2 ribs) celery, sliced
- 33g (2 TBSP) garlic paste
- 1.4L (6 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 12g (2 tsp) chicken bouillon paste (Better Than Bouillon)
- 1.5 tsp dried thyme
- 1 tsp sage, rubbed (or poultry seasoning)
- 1 small bay leaf
- 1/4 tsp sea salt, for the turkey only
- 1 tsp black pepper
- 1/4 tsp red pepper flakes

**Instructions:**

1. Make the dough exactly as in the margherita recipe — whisk the dry ingredients, add water, mix to a shaggy mass, then two rounds of stretch-and-fold 20 minutes apart. Do not divide it into balls. Oil the container, cover, and cold ferment 24-48 hours. The ferment is the only flavor this dough gets, so don't skip it; see Notes for the same-day version
2. Pull the dough from the fridge 1 hour before you cook. It will be slack and full of gas — that's fine, rolling degasses it
3. Heat the olive oil in a wide Dutch oven or 5-quart pot over medium-high. Add the turkey in one layer, season with the 1/4 tsp sea salt, and leave it alone for 4-5 minutes before breaking it up. You want real brown color on at least half of it. Break it into coarse crumbles, cook 2 minutes more, and scrape it into a bowl
4. Drop the heat to medium. Add the onion, carrot, and celery and cook 7-8 minutes, until the onion is translucent and the fond has loosened. Stir in the garlic paste, thyme, sage, black pepper, and red pepper flakes; cook 1 minute. Do not salt the vegetables
5. Whisk the bouillon paste into about 240ml of the cold bone broth until dissolved, then add that plus the remaining broth, scraping the bottom. Add the bay leaf, bring to a bare simmer, and cook uncovered 10 minutes, until the carrots are just tender. There is no roux in this version — the dumplings do all the thickening
6. While the broth simmers, flour your surface generously and roll the dough to 2-3mm — thinner than feels right. It is a stiff, high-gluten dough and it will fight back; if it snaps back on itself, walk away for 10 minutes and let the gluten relax rather than forcing it
7. Cut the sheet into rough 1" × 2" rectangles. Dust the cut pieces with more flour and toss so they don't stick to each other. That clinging flour is your thickener — do not brush it off
8. Return the turkey and its juices to the pot and bring back to a steady simmer, a little more active than a bare one. Drop the dumplings in a few at a time, stirring gently between handfuls so they never settle into a clump
9. Simmer uncovered 8-10 minutes, stirring occasionally. They're done when they've gone from matte to slightly translucent at the edges and taste chewy-tender rather than pasty in the center. The broth will thicken visibly over the last 3 minutes
10. Fish out the bay leaf and taste for salt now — this is the first and only time it needs it, since nothing in the pot came pre-salted. Add bouillon paste 1/2 tsp at a time

**Freeze:** Freeze the stew base after step 5 in Souper Cubes for up to 3 months. Cooked dumplings turn to paste on thaw, so they're always made fresh. The rolled dough freezes well as a separate ball — thaw overnight in the fridge, then roll and cut.

**Reheat:** Bring the thawed base back to a simmer, then roll, cut, and cook the dumplings from step 6.

**Storage:** Fridge, 3 days. Slick dumplings hold up better than biscuit ones but still swell — loosen with a splash of broth when reheating, covered, over low heat.

**Notes:**

- Sodium: this lands near 625mg per serving against ~1,750mg for the biscuit version. The biscuits were carrying ~4,560mg on their own; the dough replacing them carries about 780mg. Bone broth is nearly unsalted (95mg/cup), so the bouillon paste is still the only real lever
- Same-day dough: mix, stretch-and-fold twice at 20-minute intervals, then rest at room temperature 1 hour. It rolls and cooks identically — you only lose the fermented flavor, which is the entire reason for the cold ferment
- Roll it thinner than you think. At 5mm these read as gummy noodles; at 2-3mm they swell into exactly the right chew. Thickness matters more here than any other variable
- If your dough is a spare cold-fermented ball from a pizza batch, it has the full 5g salt rather than 2g. Use it — just hold back the bouillon at step 10, since it adds roughly 300mg sodium per serving
- Cook the dumplings separately in plain simmering water and add them at serving if you want leftovers that don't keep absorbing broth. You lose the thickening, so add the 24g flour roux from the biscuit version back at step 4
- Higher-protein dough: swapping 50g of the flour for another 50g vital wheat gluten adds ~5g protein per serving, but 40% gluten crosses into genuinely rubbery. 22% is the ceiling that still tastes like dough
- Half packet: 385g turkey with broth cut to 1.2L gives 4 servings at roughly 500 cal | 49g protein | 12g fat | 48g carbs, and takes the whole pot to about 2,000 cal
