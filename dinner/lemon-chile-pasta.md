### Chile and Black Pepper Pasta

**Servings:** 4
**Per serving:** 452 cal | 31g protein | 20g fat | 84g carbs
**Per 100g:** 129 cal | 9g protein | 6g fat | 24g carbs (~350g per serving)

Adapted from Andy Baraghani's fresh lemon and chile pasta, with the lemon dropped and the pepper raised to carry it instead. Creamy and spicy — butter, serrano, black pepper, and parmesan come together in minutes. Closer to a chile cacio e pepe than the original.

**Ingredients:**

- 454g (1 lb) Carbe Diem spaghetti
- 40g (3 TBSP) butter
- 10g garlic paste
- 1 small serrano chile, thinly sliced
- 1.5 tsp black pepper, freshly ground
- 30g (2 TBSP) white cooking wine
- 120ml (1/2 cup) Fairlife 2% ultra-filtered milk
- 2-3g (1/2 tsp) sodium citrate
- 130g (4.5 oz) parmesan, plus more for serving
- Sea salt to taste

**Instructions:**

1. Bring a large pot of water to a boil and season generously with salt. Cook pasta until a bit firmer than al dente, about 2 minutes less than package directions. Reserve 480ml (2 cups) pasta water, then drain
2. While the water comes to a boil, melt butter in a large pot or Dutch oven over medium heat. Add garlic paste, chile, and pepper and cook, stirring occasionally, until the mixture is very fragrant, about 2 minutes. Pour in the white cooking wine and let it cook off for 30 seconds
3. Pour in the milk with sodium citrate whisked in. Stir to combine, season lightly with salt, and bring to a gentle simmer
4. Remove from heat. Finely grate the parmesan, then gradually whisk it in until the sauce is smooth and fully emulsified. Whisk in 240ml (1 cup) of the reserved pasta water to loosen — the sauce should be slightly thinner than desired since the pasta will absorb some
5. Add the drained pasta and toss to coat. If the sauce is too thick, add more pasta water 1-2 TBSP at a time. Season with salt and pepper
6. Divide among bowls, sprinkle with more parmesan and pepper, and top with any chile slices clinging to the pot

**Serve:** Immediately while saucy — the sauce thickens as it cools.

**Notes:**

- Sodium citrate prevents the parmesan from clumping and splitting in a low-fat sauce. If you are out of it, mix 1 tsp lemon or lime juice with a pinch of baking soda, let it fizz out completely, and use that instead. It has to be citrus — citric acid plus baking soda is what makes sodium citrate, and vinegar gives you sodium acetate, which does not emulsify
- Go light on the salt until the end. Nothing here is acidic enough to cut it now, the cooking wine is salted, and between the pasta water and 130g of parmesan the dish arrives most of the way there on its own. Taste before the final seasoning in step 5
- Black pepper is doing the lifting that the lemon zest used to. Grind it fresh and bloom it in the butter in step 2 — pre-ground pepper off the shelf will read flat and dusty at this quantity
- Sauce can be made ahead without pasta and stored in the fridge for 3-4 days — reheat gently with a splash of milk or water to loosen
- Dry white wine works in place of the cooking wine at the same volume, and is a little cleaner-tasting; drop the added salt further if you use it. Shaoxing wine is what the original called for if you want to go back
