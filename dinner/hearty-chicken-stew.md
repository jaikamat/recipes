### Hearty Chicken Stew

**Servings:** 6
**Per serving:** 662 cal | 56g protein | 28g fat | 39g carbs
**Per 100g:** 116 cal | 10g protein | 5g fat | 7g carbs (~570g per serving)

Seared chicken thighs braised in the oven with potatoes, carrots, and white wine until the meat shreds itself against a spoon. Closer to chicken pot pie filling than to chicken soup, and genuinely better on day two.

**Ingredients:**

_Stew:_

- 1361g (3 lb) boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 2-3 pieces each
- 14g (about 1 TBSP) sea salt, divided
- 2 tsp black pepper
- 45ml (3 TBSP) olive oil
- 42g (3 TBSP) butter
- 200g (1 large) onion, rough chopped
- 200g (2-3 ribs) celery, diced
- 16g (1 TBSP) garlic paste
- 34g (1/4 cup) 00 flour
- 240ml (1 cup) dry white wine
- 720ml (3 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 454g (1 lb) carrots, cut into 1-2 inch lengths
- 567g (1.25 lb) Yukon Gold potatoes, cut into 4cm (1.5-2 inch) chunks
- 10ml (2 tsp) Worcestershire sauce
- 8g (about 8 sprigs) fresh thyme
- 2 bay leaves

_Finish:_

- 150g (1 cup) frozen peas
- 6g (2 tsp) cornstarch, whisked into 30ml cold water (optional, for thickening)

**Instructions:**

1. Season the chicken all over with about 10g of the sea salt and 1 tsp of the black pepper. Doing this the night before and leaving it uncovered in the fridge is a real upgrade — the salt works into the meat instead of sitting on the surface
2. Heat the oven to 325°F with a rack in the lower middle. Heat the olive oil in a 5-quart braiser or wide Dutch oven over just above medium until it shimmers and is barely smoking
3. Sear the chicken in two batches, 4-6 minutes per side, until deeply browned. The pieces shrink, so you can crowd them a little, but the further apart they start the better the color. Move each batch and its juices to a plate and tent with foil. A wide braiser beats a tall Dutch oven here — the extra bottom surface is what gets this done in two batches instead of three
4. Drop the heat to just under medium. Add the onion and celery, the butter, and a pinch of the remaining salt. The salt pulls water out of the vegetables, and that water is what lets you scrape the fond off the bottom with the flat edge of a wooden spoon. Cook about 10 minutes, until very soft but barely colored — if the pot darkens faster than the vegetables release moisture, add a TBSP of water and keep scraping
5. Stir in the garlic paste and cook 1-2 minutes, until fragrant
6. Sprinkle the flour over everything and stir constantly for about a minute, until no dry white spots remain
7. Turn the heat to high and pour in the wine. Scrape the bottom for 20-30 seconds and move quickly — it goes from liquid to thick paste fast. Whisk in the bone broth, bring to a boil, and scrape once more until the bottom of the pot is smooth
8. Return the chicken and every drop of its resting juices. Add the carrots, potatoes, Worcestershire, thyme, and bay leaves, pushing everything under the liquid as far as it will go. Cover and bake 45 minutes
9. Uncover and bake another 45-60 minutes, until the liquid has concentrated and the chicken falls apart when you push a spoon through it. The potatoes and carrots should be tender but still holding their shape — that window, not the chicken, is what sets the timing, since thighs are nearly impossible to overcook in a braise
10. Stir in the peas and bake 10 minutes more, uncovered. Add them 15 minutes out instead if they are going in frozen
11. If you want it thicker, set the pot over medium heat and drizzle in the cornstarch slurry while stirring — it sets within seconds. Cornstarch has roughly double the thickening power of flour, so add another teaspoon's worth only if it still looks thin. Simmering uncovered for 5-10 minutes does the same job without the starch
12. Fish out the bay leaves, taste for salt and pepper, and let the pot sit 10 minutes off the heat before serving

**Freeze:** Cool completely and portion into Souper Cubes or freezer bags, up to 3 months. Freeze it before the cornstarch if you can — starch-thickened liquid tends to weep on reheat, while the flour-only version holds.

**Thaw:** Overnight in the fridge.

**Reheat:** Covered, over low heat, with a splash of broth or water to loosen. It tightens a lot in the fridge.

**Serve:** Crusty bread for the bottom of the bowl. It needs no starch alongside — the potatoes are already in it.

**Storage:** Fridge, 4 days.

**Notes:**

- Thighs are the point. Breast dries out over a 90-minute braise; if that is what you have, sear it for the fond and add it back for only the last 30 minutes. Rotisserie chicken works but gives up the browning, which is most of the flavor here
- Salt: 14g against roughly 3.4kg of finished stew is about 0.4%, and almost all of it goes on the chicken at step 1. Season the vegetables with a pinch only — the broth reduces by a third in the uncovered stretch, so anything seasoned to taste early lands salty at the end
- Thyme: thin-stemmed thyme is edible and can go in loose. Woody supermarket thyme should be tied or bundled in cheesecloth so it comes out whole
- Sage and rosemary both belong here. A tsp of rubbed sage pushes it the rest of the way toward chicken pot pie filling
- No flour: skip step 6 and thicken at the end with the cornstarch, or smash a few of the cooked potatoes against the side of the pot and stir them back in
- The peas are easy to leave out. They are there for texture, since everything else in the bowl is soft
- Thighs render plenty of fat, so 30ml (2 TBSP) of olive oil is usually enough for the sear despite the 45ml called for
- Broth: Kirkland bone broth is the house liquid and carries about 33g of protein across the pot, or ~5g per serving more than a standard low-sodium chicken stock. That protein is collagen, which is incomplete — if you track toward a complete-protein target, count the honest number closer to 50g per serving
- Stovetop: cover and hold at a bare simmer on the lowest burner for the same times, checking the bottom now and then. Slow cooker: sear and build the base in a skillet, transfer everything in, and cook on low until the potatoes and carrots are tender
