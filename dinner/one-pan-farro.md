### One-Pan Farro with Tomatoes, Sausage and Kale

**Servings:** 4 (about 360g each)
**Per serving:** 480 cal | 26g protein | 19g fat | 53g carbs
**Per 100g:** 133 cal | 7g protein | 5g fat | 15g carbs (~360g per serving)

A summer one-pan farro built on the pilaf playbook rather than a boil. The sausage is seared hard and pulled out, the tomatoes blister cut-side down in its rendered fat, and the farro toasts in the fond before any liquid goes in. The sausage returns at the end so it never simmers grey.

**Ingredients:**

- 14g (1 TBSP) olive oil
- 227g (1/2 lb) Italian sausage, casings removed
- 340g (12 oz) cherry tomatoes, halved
- 100g onion, chopped (about 1/2 large)
- 3 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- 1/2 tsp red pepper flakes
- 240g (1 1/4 cups) semi-pearled farro
- 600g (2 1/2 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 8g sea salt, divided — see Notes
- 80g kale, stems removed and torn into large pieces (about 5 large leaves)
- 5g basil, cut into thin ribbons (about 10 leaves)
- Olive oil for drizzling
- 40g parmesan, grated, for serving (optional)

**Instructions:**

1. Heat the olive oil in a 12-inch skillet or wide Dutch oven over medium-high — hot enough to sizzle, not smoking. Add the sausage and press it into a flat slab covering the pan floor. Leave it completely alone for 4-5 minutes. You want a hard, dark crust on the underside, because that stuck-on fond is the entire flavor base of the dish. Break it into coarse crumbles, cook 2 minutes more, and scrape it into a bowl, leaving the rendered fat behind
2. Lay the tomatoes cut-side down in the fat in a single layer and leave them undisturbed 3-4 minutes, until the cut faces are browned and collapsing. Scrape them into the bowl with the sausage along with any juices
3. Drop the heat to medium. Add the onion and a pinch of the salt and cook 4-5 minutes, until softened and translucent — the moisture it gives up will start loosening the fond, so scrape as you go. Add the garlic and red pepper flakes and cook 1 minute more, until fragrant
4. Add the farro and stir constantly for 2-3 minutes, until the grains are coated in fat and smell nutty and toasted. This is the step the original recipe trades away for a presoak, and it is worth far more than the soak was
5. Pour in the broth and add all but about 2g of the remaining salt. Scrape the bottom hard as it comes up to a boil — every bit of fond should lift and dissolve into the liquid. Reduce to a gentle simmer, uncovered
6. Simmer 25 minutes, stirring every 8-10 minutes and scraping the bottom so the farro doesn't catch. If the pan goes dry before the grain is tender, add broth or water 80g at a time
7. Return the tomatoes and their juices and simmer 5-10 minutes more, until the farro is tender with a meaty chew and the liquid has almost fully absorbed — moist, not soupy. Pile the kale on top for the last 5 minutes and fold it in as it wilts
8. Kill the heat and fold in the sausage crumbles. Taste and add the reserved salt only if it needs it — the sausage carries a lot. Rest 5 minutes uncovered so the last of the liquid absorbs evenly, then scatter with basil, drizzle with olive oil, and finish with parmesan

**Freeze:** Cool completely and portion into containers for up to 2 months. The farro and sausage freeze well; the tomatoes and kale go soft, so this is better fresh than frozen.

**Reheat:** Microwave from frozen with a splash of broth, covered, stirring halfway. Finish with fresh basil and a drizzle of olive oil to bring it back.

**Serve:** Good warm or at room temperature, and it holds up as a cold grain salad the next day.

**Storage:** Fridge, 4 days. The farro keeps drinking liquid, so it tightens noticeably by day 2.

**Notes:**

- The sausage comes out and goes back in for the same reason the turkey does in Turkey & Dumplings — 30 minutes of simmering turns a seared crumble grey and pebbly. Its rendered fat and fond stay in the pan, which is where the flavor actually lives, so nothing is lost by pulling the meat itself
- Blistering the tomatoes cut-side down is the single biggest upgrade over the original, which just boils them raw. Cherry tomatoes are high enough in sugar to caramelize properly, and that browning is what gives the dish jammy depth instead of a thin stewed-tomato note
- Toasting the grain replaces the original's presoak — you cannot toast wet farro, so it is one or the other. Toasting wins: raw farro is a neutral starch, toasted farro is a flavor, and the grains hold their edges better through a long simmer
- Broth instead of water is the other free upgrade, and it moves protein from 19g to 26g per serving. Be honest with that number though: about 7g of it is collagen from the bone broth, which is incomplete protein. Against a leucine target the real figure is nearer 19g
- Salt strategy: 8g total, but hold back roughly 2g until step 8. Italian sausage runs about 750mg sodium per 100g, so the half pound alone contributes ~1,700mg before you add anything. Kirkland bone broth is nearly unsalted, which is why it can be the liquid here. As written this lands near 1,200mg per serving
- The 2:1 broth-to-farro ratio is tight for an uncovered simmer, and toasted grain drinks slightly slower than raw. Check at 20 minutes and top up rather than letting it catch
- Protein knob: swap in turkey Italian sausage and double it to 454g (1 lb) for about 36g protein and 14g fat per serving at roughly the same calories. Bump the olive oil to 28g (2 TBSP) if you do — lean turkey renders far less fat than pork, and the pan needs it to build fond and toast the farro
- Flavor levers worth having: a parmesan rind dropped in at step 5 and fished out at the end, the basil stems (not the leaves) simmered with the broth, and a squeeze of lemon off the heat. After 30 minutes of simmering the dish reads sweet and round, and the acid is what snaps it back
- The original ingredient list says minced garlic while the method says thinly sliced — sliced holds up better here, and it goes in after the onion so it never sits on a hot dry pan
- Real time is closer to 50 minutes than the 35 the original claims, but only about 12 minutes of that is hands-on
