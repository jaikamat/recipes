### Andouille and Peppers over Cheddar Grits

**Servings:** 2
**Per serving:** 679 cal | 48g protein | 31g fat | 50g carbs
**Per 100g:** 104 cal | 7g protein | 5g fat | 8g carbs (~650g per serving)

The jambalaya pulled apart into its two best halves. Andouille gets browned hard and then smothered with peppers and onions until the whole skillet goes jammy, and it lands on grits loosened with milk and finished with cheddar. Same Cajun flavor, half the pot-watching, and the grits do the job the rice used to.

**Ingredients:**

_Sausage and peppers:_

- 302g (10 oz) Amylu paleo chicken andouille, sliced diagonally ⅜" thick
- 5ml (1 tsp) olive oil
- 100g onion, sliced ⅜" (about 1 small)
- 100g bell pepper, sliced ⅜" (about 1 small, red or green)
- 50g poblano pepper, sliced ⅜" (about 1/2)
- 55g celery, sliced ⅜" (about 1-2 stalks)
- 5g (1 tsp) garlic paste
- 3/4 tsp Cajun seasoning
- 1/4 tsp smoked paprika
- 1/4 tsp dried thyme
- 10g (2 tsp) tomato paste
- 160ml (2/3 cup) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 2g (3/4 tsp) cornstarch, whisked into 10ml cold water
- 1 green onion, sliced thin
- Sea salt and black pepper to taste

_Cheddar grits:_

- 320ml (1 1/3 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 160ml (2/3 cup) Fairlife 2% ultra-filtered milk
- 85g (1/2 cup) stone-ground grits
- 38g (1/3 cup) cheddar, shredded
- 9g (2 tsp) butter
- Sea salt and black pepper to taste

**Instructions:**

1. Start the grits first — they want 25-30 minutes and they are forgiving about sitting. Bring the 1 1/3 cups broth and the milk to a bare simmer in a heavy 2-quart saucepan. Rain the grits in while whisking, which is the only way to keep them from seizing into lumps, and whisk until the pot thickens slightly
2. Drop the heat as low as it goes, cover, and cook 25-30 minutes, whisking every 5 minutes and scraping the corners of the pot where they catch. A small pot scorches faster than a big one, so stay honest about the low heat. They are done when a spoonful holds its shape for a second before slumping. Keep them covered off the heat until the sausage is ready
3. Meanwhile, heat the olive oil in a 10" cast iron or carbon steel skillet over medium-high until it shimmers. Brown the andouille in a single layer, 3-4 minutes per side, until the cut faces are crusted and the fat has rendered out. Move it to a plate — it finishes cooking in the smother
4. Turn the heat to just above medium and add the onion, bell pepper, poblano, and celery with a pinch of salt to the rendered fat. Cook 10-12 minutes, scraping the fond up as the vegetables release water, until everything is slumped and the onion has gone translucent and a little browned at the edges. This is where the dish is made, so take the full time
5. Stir in the garlic paste, Cajun seasoning, smoked paprika, and thyme and cook about a minute, until fragrant. Add the tomato paste and keep stirring another minute, until it darkens from red to brick and stops smelling raw
6. Pour in the 2/3 cup broth, scrape the bottom smooth, and return the sausage and its juices. Simmer uncovered 6-8 minutes, until the liquid has reduced by about half and clings to the peppers
7. Stir in the cornstarch slurry and cook 30 seconds more — it goes from brothy to glossy almost immediately. Taste for salt and pepper, going easy, since both the andouille and the Cajun seasoning bring their own
8. Finish the grits: whisk in the butter, then the cheddar in two additions off the heat so it melts instead of breaking. Loosen with a splash of milk or broth if they have tightened past spoonable
9. Spoon the grits into two wide bowls, pile the sausage and peppers in the center with plenty of the pan sauce, and scatter the green onion over the top

**Freeze:** Freeze the sausage and peppers alone, up to 3 months. Grits turn grainy and weep after a freeze, so make them fresh — they are a 30-minute component with no technique to them.

**Thaw:** Overnight in the fridge.

**Reheat:** Sausage and peppers: covered, over medium-low, with a splash of broth. Grits: in a saucepan over low with 60-120ml milk per portion, whisking until they come back together. They set into a sliceable block in the fridge and will not loosen without added liquid and patience.

**Serve:** Hot sauce at the table, and a vinegary green — collards or a sharp slaw — to cut the richness.

**Storage:** Fridge, 4 days, components stored separately.

**Notes:**

- Scales cleanly by 3 to feed six, which is the version worth cooking if you want leftovers — the smother only gets better on day two, and a 12" skillet handles 907g (2 lb) of sausage in two batches
- Stone-ground grits are the whole point of the texture; they taste like corn where instant grits taste like paste. Quick grits work in a pinch at 5-7 minutes and the same liquid ratio, but skip anything labeled instant
- Grits to liquid is 1:6 by volume here, which lands creamy rather than stiff. Pull back to 1:5 if you want them to hold a spoon upright
- Cooking the grits in broth instead of water is most of the savory depth, and it adds about 7g of protein per serving. That protein is collagen, so count it honestly if you track complete protein
- Amylu chicken andouille keeps this lean. Pork andouille runs roughly 320 cal and 27g fat per 100g against the chicken's 165 and 10.5, so it adds about 230 cal and 25g fat per serving. It also renders more fat to cook the vegetables in, so skip the olive oil
- Shrimp is the natural addition. Add 115g (4 oz) peeled shrimp at step 7, off the heat and covered, for 3-4 minutes
- The vegetables want to be sliced, not diced. Diced reads as jambalaya trinity; sliced reads as sausage and peppers, which is the dish you are after
- Cheddar can go. Butter alone gives cleaner corn flavor, and drops about 75 cal and 6g fat per serving
