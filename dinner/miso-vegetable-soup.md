### Miso Vegetable Soup

**Servings:** 4
**Per serving:** 330 cal | 26g protein | 13g fat | 30g carbs
**Per 100g:** 67 cal | 5g protein | 3g fat | 6g carbs (~495g per serving)

Adapted from Hikari Miso's vegetable miso soup. Potato-and-onion miso soup is a Japanese home-cooking staple; this version bulks it up with carrots and a full block of firm tofu, and swaps the water for unsalted bone broth. The broth does double duty: it gives the soup a savory backbone plain water can't, and it's where a little over 40% of the protein comes from. Because it's unsalted, the miso is the only real seasoning, which makes salt easy to control — weigh the miso and adjust at the end. Protein is close to a third of the calories.

**Ingredients:**

_Soup:_

- 30ml (2 TBSP) olive oil
- 150g onion, diced (about 1 medium)
- 350g Yukon Gold potatoes, peeled and cut into 2cm (3/4 in) pieces (about 2 medium)
- 130g carrots, peeled and cut into 6mm (1/4 in) coins (about 2 medium)
- 960ml (4 cups) Kirkland chicken bone broth
- 400g (14 oz) firm tofu, drained and cut into 2cm (3/4 in) cubes

_To finish:_

- 72g (4 TBSP) white miso (Hikari Organic)
- 30g green onions, thinly sliced (about 2)
- 4g (1 snack pack) Chung Jung One perilla oil seaweed, crumbled (optional)

**Instructions:**

1. Cut the potatoes into 2cm pieces and the carrots into thin 6mm coins. Carrots cook slower than potatoes, so cutting them thinner means both finish at the same time. Drain the tofu, pat it dry, and cube it
2. Heat the oil in a 4- to 5-quart soup pot over medium. Add the onion and cook 5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until translucent
3. Add the potatoes and carrots and cook 3 more minutes, stirring, until everything is coated and the edges start to soften
4. Pour in the broth, scraping the bottom clean, and bring to a boil. Boil 12-15 minutes, until a fork slides through the largest potato piece and the carrots with no resistance. Start checking at 12
5. Drop the heat to low so the soup is barely moving. Slide in the tofu and let it warm through for 3 minutes. Stir gently, if at all — firm tofu holds its shape but breaks apart if you churn it
6. Put the miso in a ladle, lower it partway into the broth, and whisk with a spoon or chopsticks until fully dissolved, then stir it through the pot. Start with 60g, taste, and add the last 12g only if it needs it — more so if you are also finishing with the seasoned seaweed, which brings its own salt. From this point on the soup should not boil, since boiling drives off the miso's aroma and leaves it tasting flat
7. Ladle into bowls and top with the green onions and seaweed at the table. The seaweed goes soft within a minute or two in hot broth, so it never goes in the pot

**Freeze:** Not recommended. Potatoes turn mealy and tofu goes spongy and chewy after freezing.

**Reheat:** On the stove over medium-low until steaming, stirring gently, or microwave at 70% power, stirring halfway. Don't let it boil — reheating hits the miso the same way as step 6. Add fresh garnish after reheating.

**Storage:** Fridge, 3-4 days. Keep the seaweed in its pack, not in the containers. The potatoes absorb some broth overnight and the miso settles to the bottom, so stir before reheating and loosen with a splash of water if it's thicker than you want.

**Notes:**

- The broth is unsalted, so the miso is the only real seasoning. Hikari Organic White Miso runs 820mg sodium per 18g, and 72g across 4 servings works out to 18g a bowl — about 820mg per serving from the miso alone. The seaweed adds a little more
- Macros assume Kirkland unsalted chicken bone broth at about 45 cal and 11g protein per cup. Other brands run anywhere from 6g to 10g protein per cup, and every 1g per cup of difference moves the protein by 1g per serving, so check your label
- Macros exclude the optional seaweed, which adds about 6 cal per serving
- The per-100g figures assume some evaporation during the boil. For exact numbers, weigh the finished soup (minus the pot) and divide
- **Fat knob:** cutting the oil to 15ml takes it to about 300 cal and 9g fat per serving. The onion still softens fine; stir a little more often
- **Protein knob:** a second 400g block of firm tofu takes it to about 36g protein per serving for an extra 90 cal. Super-firm or high-protein tofu at the same weight adds about 5-6g protein per serving for about 50 more calories; plain extra-firm is close enough to firm that it barely moves the numbers
- **Volume knob:** 150g of baby spinach or chopped bok choy stirred in with the tofu adds about 40g per serving for under 10 cal
- Chopped leek can stand in for the green onions as the finishing allium
