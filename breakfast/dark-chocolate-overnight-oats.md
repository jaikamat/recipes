### Dark Chocolate Overnight Oats

**Servings:** 6
**Per serving:** 354 cal | 20g protein | 8g fat | 57g carbs

A MUSH-style overnight oats with dark cocoa, whole dates, and toasted oats, batched for a week of breakfasts. No date paste to make ahead — the dates and cocoa steep together in hot water right in the blender jar, then the dairy goes in on low speed so the mixture stays dense instead of foamy.

**Ingredients:**

- 120g (1 1/2 cups) rolled oats
- 12 Medjool dates, pitted
- 60g (3/4 cup) dutch process cocoa powder
- 30g (2 TBSP) erythritol
- 1 1/2 tsp decaf instant coffee
- 1/2 tsp sea salt
- 180ml (3/4 cup) hot water
- 590g (2 1/2 cups) 4% cottage cheese
- 400ml (1 2/3 cups) Fairlife 2% ultra-filtered milk
- 6mL (1 1/2 tsp) vanilla extract

**Instructions:**

1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Spread the rolled oats in a single layer on a sheet tray and toast for 8–10 minutes, stirring once halfway through, until they smell nutty and have taken on a light golden color. Set them aside to cool completely — they'll be ready by the time the blender work is done
2. Put the dates, cocoa powder, erythritol, instant coffee, and salt in the blender jar and pour the hot water over them. Let it sit for 10 minutes — the dates soften, the cocoa blooms, the coffee and erythritol dissolve, all in the same rest
3. Blend on high until completely smooth, about 1 minute, using the tamper to keep the dates moving. There should be no flecks of skin left
4. Let the jar sit uncovered for about 10 minutes, until the base is warm rather than hot, so the cottage cheese doesn't turn grainy when it goes in
5. Add the cottage cheese, milk, and vanilla. Blend on the lowest setting and ramp up only as far as you need to pull everything smooth, about 30 seconds — high speed here whips air in and gives you foam that never fully settles
6. Divide the cooled oats among six jars, 20g (1/4 cup) each. Pour the blended mixture over them, dividing evenly, about 255g per jar
7. Stir each jar, then tap them firmly on the counter a few times to release trapped bubbles. Seal and refrigerate — overnight at minimum, but a full 24 hours is better

**Serve:** Stir well before eating. Enjoy cold straight from the fridge.

**Storage:** Keeps in the fridge for up to a week, which is the point of the batch.

**Notes:**

- Toasting is the one step here that costs an extra pan, and it earns it. Raw oats are a neutral thickener; toasted oats are a flavor, and the Maillard notes give the cocoa something to sit against instead of just sweetness. They also hold their chew better across a week in the fridge
- Weigh the 120g before toasting, not after — toasting drives off a little moisture, so post-toast weight would overshoot. It's worth toasting a double or triple batch while the oven is on and keeping the extra in an airtight jar
- Let the oats cool completely before the mixture goes over them. Warm oats start gelatinizing on contact and set up pasty in the jar
- The vanilla goes in cold with the dairy, not into the hot date base — its aromatics are volatile and blending them into a near-boiling jar drives most of them off
- Cocoa's aroma compounds are fat-soluble, so 4% cottage cheese carries the chocolate noticeably better than 0% does. Swapping back to 0% saves ~32 cal and 5g fat per serving but costs real depth — it reads thinner and slightly dusty
- Twelve dates is roughly the sweetness the 1.5 date paste cubes per serving gave in the single-serving version, without the separate paste-making step
- Blending in ingredient order matters more than usual here: dates want high speed and heat, dairy wants low speed and no heat. One pass on high gets you a foamy, slightly grainy result
- Don't add more salt to the mix — the cottage cheese is already carrying about 2g of sodium across the batch, which puts it near proper dessert seasoning on its own
- The cottage cheese and milk are balanced as a trade, not as independent dials — 100g of cottage cheese swapped for 100ml of milk thins the set jar while keeping total volume, jar size, and the liquid-to-oat ratio where they are. Cutting cottage cheese without adding milk back mostly shrinks the jars: the oats stay at 20g each, so there's less liquid per oat and the result sets about as thick anyway
- If it's still thicker than you like after a full 24 hours, add milk on top rather than cutting more cheese — 450ml (1 3/4 cups) puts jars at about 305g, so either take the bigger portion or split into seven. Don't drop below about 540g cottage cheese; past that the chocolate starts reading thin for the same reason the 0% swap does
- Judge the texture at 24 hours, not overnight. The oats are still absorbing at the 12-hour mark, and a jar that seems right then will be noticeably thicker by day three
- A 64oz Vitamix jar is about two-thirds full at the end, which is where it vortexes least and pulls in the least air. In a smaller jar, blend the dairy in two batches rather than filling it to the top
- The decaf instant coffee is there to deepen the chocolate, not to taste like coffee — 1 1/2 tsp across six jars sits under the threshold where you'd name it. Espresso powder works in the same amount, and regular caffeinated instant is fine if a morning caffeine hit is welcome
- Natural (non-alkalized) cocoa works too, for a sharper, more acidic chocolate
- Try 30g chia seeds across the batch for a thicker pudding-like mouthfeel (~25 cal per serving) — stir into the jars with the oats, not into the blender
- Try 6-12g white miso paste for umami depth — a pastry chef trick that makes chocolate taste richer. Blend it in with the dates
- Try a pinch of cardamom for a floral, exotic warmth that pairs with dates
- Try fresh orange zest for a classic French chocolate pairing
- Try a tiny pinch of cayenne for Mexican hot chocolate warmth
- Try flaky finishing salt (Maldon) on top at serving instead of mixing salt in for texture contrast
